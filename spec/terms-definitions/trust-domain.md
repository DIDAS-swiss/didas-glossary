[[def: trust domain, trust domains, trust space, trust spaces, Vertrauensraum, Vertrauensräume, espace de confiance, spazio di fiducia]]

~ A governance-defined scope in which actors apply a common set of rules for evaluating identifiers, roles, [[ref: credential]] types, authorisations, trust information and acceptance policies. Multiple trust domains can coexist on the same [[ref: trust infrastructure]] — sectoral, federal or international — each with its own [[ref: governance]], its own acceptance rules and, where applicable, its own trust statements.

~ A trust domain is to be distinguished from the [[ref: trust infrastructure]] (the federal technical and organisational basis) and from the [[ref: ecosystem]] (the totality of actors building on it). A trust domain becomes concretely visible where a [[ref: governing authority]] publishes via the trust infrastructure which actors may issue which credential types.

~ DIDAS governance guidance (community proposal, not asserted by a swiyu specification): complementary registries of a trust domain's own are possible, provided they remain connected to the Confederation's trust infrastructure; the governing-authority role and registry operation lie with the respectively competent bodies.

~ Relation to ToIP: related but not equivalent — [[xref: toip-glossary, trust-domain]]. The upstream definition is technology-oriented (a security domain defined by an architecture, security policy or trust community); this entry is the Swiss governance definition. Where the distinction matters, "swiyu trust domain" may be used. Nearby upstream: [[xref: toip-glossary, trust-context]] and [[xref: toip-glossary, trust-community]].
