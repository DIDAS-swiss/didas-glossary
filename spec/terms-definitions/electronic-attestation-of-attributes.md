[[def: electronic attestation of attributes, electronic attestations of attributes, EAA, EAAs]]

~ Normative space: eIDAS 2 / EU ARF. This entry records a term of the EU framework for interoperability mapping. Nothing in it applies normatively in the swiyu context.

~ Definition in the eIDAS Regulation as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, as carried verbatim in [ARF Annex 1](https://github.com/eu-digital-identity-wallet/eudi-doc-architecture-and-reference-framework/blob/main/docs/annexes/annex-1/annex-1-definitions.md): "An attestation in electronic form that allows attributes to be authenticated." An attribute is "a characteristic, quality, right or permission of a natural or legal person or of an object".

~ The ARF uses *attestation*, when not further qualified, as "a collective term for a QEAA, PuB-EAA, or (non-qualified) EAA". A PuB-EAA is an electronic attestation of attributes issued by a public sector body responsible for an authentic source, or by a public sector body designated by the Member State to issue such attestations on its behalf (Article 45f and Annex VII of the Regulation). See [[ref: qualified electronic attestation of attributes]].

~ Swiss counterpart: [[ref: electronic credential]], where the relationship is classified.

~ Does not imply: recording this term does not make eIDAS or ARF requirements applicable in the swiyu context, and it does not imply that Swiss electronic credentials are EAAs in the legal sense of the Regulation.

~ Relation to ToIP: related but not equivalent — [[xref: toip-glossary, attestation]], a signed statement that fulfilment of specified requirements has been demonstrated. An EAA is specifically an attestation that allows attributes to be authenticated, and carries the legal categories of the Regulation.
